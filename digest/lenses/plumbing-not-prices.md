LENS: the plumbing of money, not the price of it.

Score FIT 0–3 on whether the item describes a change in how money moves: who may lend to
whom, what settles where, which claims are senior, what a rule now permits or forbids.

3 — A rule, a pipe or a claim structure changed. Examples: a central bank changes its
    operating framework or its collateral rules; a clearing, settlement or custody
    arrangement moves; a capital control, or its removal; a change in who is allowed to
    lend, hold or issue; a restructuring that reorders who gets paid first; a currency's
    role in invoicing or reserves shifting for a stated reason.
2 — Meaningful movement inside the existing machinery: a supervisory rule with
    second-order effects, a large issuer changing where or how it funds itself, a market
    maker or exchange changing what it will hold.
1 — Interesting but mostly the level: a rate decision reported as a number, an index at
    a record, an earnings beat or miss, a forecast revision — UNLESS the item explains
    what the change does to the plumbing.
0 — Stock tips, personalities, market colour, predictions, opinion pieces without a
    mechanism, crypto price moves.

KIND:
  plumbing — the item is about how money moves (rules, claims, settlement, access)
  level    — the item is about how much things cost (prices, rates, indices, earnings)
  neither  — off-lens entirely

NOVELTY 0–3: 3 = a new fact about the world; 0 = another episode of an ongoing story
with nothing structurally new.

MECHANISM: name the causal machinery in ≤12 words, e.g. "collateral haircut change
forcing a funding shift", "settlement moving to a different clearing house", "capital
control redirecting export receipts onshore". Null if there is none.

Bias notes: A change in a small market counts if the mechanism is general. Ignore the
source's own framing of importance.
