Weekly Digest · open-source design · UI mockup

Weekly Digest interface

What the local web app looks like on first run, on a normal Friday, and when reviewing a week · 4 September 2026

The app runs at http://127.0.0.1:8765 in the user's own browser. Every screen below is drawn with real content from the repository's fixtures and a real September week, so the proportions are honest: how many items a run touches, how long the audit list is, what a carried entry looks like next to a written one.

Two rules the screens are built around. The user never sees a temperature, a seed, a batch size or the word "rubric" — the UI says "lens", "filter" and "write". And there is no button to override the filter: the way to change what gets in is to teach the lens on the calibration screen, so the audit for any past week still reproduces.

Part I · Orientation

How to read this mockup

Each section is one screen or one flow, drawn as a browser window with the app's left-hand navigation. Sidebar links are the six screens the design names: This week, Review, Lens, Feeds, Settings, About. Setup is a wizard that appears only until it is finished, then is reachable from Settings.

The style is the same palette digest/emit.py already uses for the emitted edition, so the app and the briefing it produces look like one thing. Nothing loads from the network; fonts are system stacks.

The weekly digest, week 36. Three of this week's items share one mechanism: a state deciding where money may go. Japan's central bank, Indonesia's export earnings and Brussels' steel safeguard each change a rule rather than a price. The rest stand alone.

First, East Asia.

The Bank of Japan rewrites its operating framework

Japan's central bank has replaced the yield target it held for nine years with a corridor on the overnight rate, and will let the ten-year bond find its own level. Banks that had parked reserves at the central bank now earn less for doing so, so the money has somewhere else to go. The change sits inside a broader retreat from the tools adopted after the 2008 crisis.

The new corridor pays banks less to sit still, which is the point.

Economist — Finance and Economics · FT — World

Indonesia holds nickel export earnings onshore

In Semafor's own words.

Indonesia will require exporters of nickel and other processed minerals to keep their foreign-currency earnings in domestic banks for a full year, up from three months, the finance ministry said on Tuesday. The rule applies to shipments from October and covers roughly twelve billion dollars a year in receipts. Officials said the aim was to deepen the domestic dollar market and reduce the rupiah's dependence on portfolio inflows. Miners had lobbied for a shorter period.

Part V · Boundaries

What the UI deliberately does not do
There is no button to keep or drop a single item.

The stored classifications plus the pure select function are what let audit reproduce any past week. An override would be a second source of truth. The review panel's "Something missing? Teach the lens" link goes to calibration, which turns the miss into a labelled example.

The edition's text cannot be edited in the app.

The app produces a briefing; it is not a document editor. The .txt and .md files are on disk for anyone who wants to change words before sending them on.

Temperature, seed, batch size, thinking, context size and pacing are not on any screen.

Each has a measured right value recorded in digest.toml's comments. They live under [advanced] in config.toml for anyone who wants them. Thinking is detected from Ollama's capability list rather than set.

The four machinery prompts are not shown.

classify.md, cluster.md and the two synthesize_*.md files stay in the package. Only the lens is the user's.

It listens on 127.0.0.1 only, with no accounts and no remote mode.

One person, one machine. The scheduled job shares the same core and needs no browser open.

Google Drive is under Settings → Advanced, not in setup.

The OAuth path requires creating a Google Cloud project. The default delivery is the output folder, which every user already has.
